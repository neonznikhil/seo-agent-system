The multiplier method is the most common approach. It works by multiplying your total medical bills by a number between 1.5 and 5. Minor injuries often use a lower multiplier, while severe, long-term injuries justify a higher one. Documentation is what determines where you fall on that scale, so consistent records directly affect the final number you can claim. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statutory limitation period for accident claims?

In most jurisdictions, personal injury claims have a statutory limitation period of two to three years from the date of the collision to file a lawsuit in civil court.

What happens if you miss the statutory limitation deadline?

If you miss the statutory limitation deadline, the court will dismiss your claim with prejudice, permanently barring you from recovering financial compensation.

Can the statutory limitation period be extended or paused?

Yes, under specific tolling doctrines such as when the victim is a minor, the defendant left the state, or the injury could not be reasonably discovered immediately.

Does negotiating with an insurance adjuster pause the deadline?

No, ongoing insurance negotiations do not pause or extend the statutory limitation period. You must file a lawsuit before the statutory deadline regardless of pending settlement talks.
